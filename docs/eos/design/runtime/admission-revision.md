# R03 governed actuator admission design

[中文](../../../../docs_cn/eos/design/runtime/admission-revision_cn.md) · [Owner contract](../../interfaces/grpc.md#r03-governed-actuator-admission-target-contract) · [Hardware contract](../../interfaces/hardware.md#r03-dispatcher-admission-target-contract) · [First-use replay](../../develop/admission-revision.md) · [Frozen cases](../../../../tests/fixtures/r03_admission_revision/README.md)

**R03 qualified on merged EOS and IsOS owners.** R03 extends the merged R02 gateway and existing durable Dispatcher. It does not publish another protobuf service, execute a graph wire, implement R04 host skills or R07 browser result transport, or claim the R05/R06 physical MuJoCo fence and stop order. IsOS remains the authority for saved graph bytes and its active revision; EOS owns the admitted projection, live health, command admission and journal.

## Freeze one active generation

IsOS verifies its schema-2 generated snapshot before launching EOS. The selected leaf is `data/generated/<deployment>/v2/<revision>/<generation>/`, with `manifest.json` at the leaf and `eos/graph.json` beneath it. In the managed path, EOS requires its profile path to name that listed regular file, rejects symlinks and path escape, verifies its just-read bytes against the manifest's SHA-256 entry, and retains the manifest's lowercase 64-character `revision` as the IsOS saved whole-graph `active_revision`. The manifest generation and selected leaf must pass IsOS's existing verification before spawn; EOS repeats the file and revision checks before gRPC/control readiness. The existing Vision/route-status `projection_sha256` remains the digest of EOS projection bytes and is never an alias for the saved revision. Managed mode requires both `EOS_CONTROL_GRANTS_FILE` and a selected profile path with the IsOS `/v2/<revision>/<generation>/eos/graph.json` layout. One without the other fails startup. With both inputs, EOS requires an exact verifier `run_id` match to its effective `--run-id`; an absent, malformed, symlinked or mismatched schema-2 manifest or verifier fails startup before adapter construction, listener bind or ready event. The optional public `--run-id` alone is only a correlation value, including for standalone tests. IsOS can still inspect and explicitly migrate legacy saved graphs without starting this actuator path. A standalone/mock profile with neither managed input may serve read-only discovery with its projection digest and movement capability unavailable, even if `--run-id` is supplied, but cannot claim an IsOS `active_revision` or enable a managed actuator write.

The current runtime has one immutable graph per process. Every actuator ingress compares the caller's expected active revision to this admitted value at the command's final release gate, after any queue wait. A saved draft changed without Restart does not alter EOS's active value. During Restart, the old listener quiesces and new writes fail before the new listener publishes a complete generation; a stale client must rediscover and use the new active revision. There is no in-process graph hot swap. Legacy `1.0.0` graphs and the old generated layout without a schema-2 manifest remain safely readable for inspection and explicit migration, but that layout has no verified saved active revision for R03 production actuator writes; those writes fail closed until the graph is applied through the revisioned layout. Historical graph and protobuf identities remain intact.

## Bind authority to the caller

Loopback TCP, a supplied source path and a graph edge do not authenticate a process. For managed runs IsOS creates independent OS-cryptographic-RNG per-run `egia-control` and `operator-control` grants in its private runtime directory, passes only the intended grant path to supervised EGIA or the EOS browser bridge, and gives EOS the verifier path through `EOS_CONTROL_GRANTS_FILE` as defined in the owner contract. IsOS grant issuance and launch handoff are defined in its owner `interfaces/` contract and merged through IsOS PR #52; the post-merge clean-home replay verified their handoff to EOS. Neither graph bytes nor generated configuration, capability descriptors, logs or public metadata contain a grant. EOS validates a Bearer grant in constant time and maps it to a fixed role and scope; same-UID processes remain within the local OS trust boundary. A missing, wrong, expired or cross-run grant fails before reservation or adapter release. The EOS fixture injected deterministic grants for focused tests; the merged IsOS owner slice and post-merge clean-home replay supplied production-boundary evidence. Discovery and read RPCs remain available to the existing EGIA typed client without a grant. A02 adds EGIA write metadata after it has a managed grant. The merged EOS bridge injects its private operator grant and rejects browser-supplied grant bytes; R07 still owns full browser-path and result-follow-up qualification.

`egia-control` may submit movement only through governed gRPC; `operator-control` may submit movement only through the private `primitive_typed` bridge path, while its gRPC movement Submit is denied. Both resolve an exact active primitive binding at Dispatcher. EOS verifies the binding and role, while A02 must prove EGIA safety-stage routing before EGIA submits. `egia-control` may request a normal robot-wide `robot.stop`; both roles may request `motion.stop` for an exact admitted device binding and may invoke `safety.estop` with their valid grant even when the expected revision is missing or stale; emergency stop cannot be blocked by a draft mismatch or an exhausted movement queue. Only `operator-control` may authorize a fresh-ID robot-wide recovery stop during movement inhibit, with an explicit `x-eos-stop-recovery: true` marker; it must cover the union of unresolved old and current targets and cannot clear the emergency latch. `egia-control` and an unmarked operator stop fail that recovery gate. There is no third public safety grant. Internal shutdown uses the Dispatcher safety seam independently of user grants. No grant authorizes a raw hardware path, graph-authored skill sequence, or an undeclared primitive. Role and target authorization are checked from the admitted registry, not client strings.

## Admission and durable identity

Each public movement ingress constructs one canonical command envelope: command ID, IsOS deployment `run_id`, caller role, server-attested ingress, saved active revision, primitive/version, exact binding source, exact hardware target, typed arguments and a separately checked monotonic deadline. The same Dispatcher method reserves this identity durably before any possible physical release. The credential bytes and transport request ID are omitted from the durable signature; the stable role/scope and ingress, IsOS `run_id` and active revision are included, while the per-attempt deadline is excluded from the duplicate signature. An EOS child restart in the same IsOS deployment run retains its run ID and grants, and a retry with the same ID and canonical envelope returns the retained state. A new IsOS deployment run rotates run ID and grants; an old grant fails, a new grant cannot query an old-run result as this run, and reuse of an old ID conflicts without release; changed revision, role, binding, target or arguments conflicts and never releases. Lookup of a retained old operation is allowed to its authorized owner even after a new graph is active, but a fresh command cannot use the old revision. An `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` release is terminal for that ID and never automatically retried. If old journal records lack the new identity fields, they remain sealed rather than being inferred from the current graph.

Stop records persist the same owner role and run ID plus distinct `MotionStop`/`RobotStop`/`SafetyEstop` kind, exact device or robot target, saved revision (nullable only for authenticated estop), and recovery marker. A same-ID cross-role stop or changed recovery marker conflicts rather than returning another role's old result. Get/Watch/Cancel require the journaled role/scope and same run; an older-run result is not exposed as current-run success. After reservation, Dispatcher checks the release coordinator, safety latch and inhibit, exact binding and adapter kind, active revision, role grant, node lifecycle and fresh health, required typed observation, finite numeric bounds and configured units, and the remaining monotonic deadline. It persists `Releasing` before calling the adapter. An expired queued command or a failed pre-release journal write calls no adapter. A terminal result is persisted with the same command ID; ambiguous physical release, lost terminal persistence or a crash after `Releasing` becomes `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and inhibits movement until the existing explicit stop recovery proves quiescence. Do not convert a transport timeout or disconnection into physical success or cancellation. `motion.stop`, `robot.stop` and `safety.estop` retain their distinct R02 journaled kinds and reserved stop lanes.

Remove production access to the raw private-socket `dispatch`, legacy `manual` and `primitive`, graph sequence `skill_execute`, `skill_result`, unjournaled `stop` and `manual_stop`, and raw `cancel`, `result` and `skill_cancel` routes. `GetOperation`, `WatchOperation`, `CancelOperation` and the later R07 private result follow-up require a valid grant matching the journaled role/scope of the ID; cross-role access is denied without revealing or changing the operation. `skill_cancel` cannot invoke unjournaled `stop_node` in production. Owner-only shutdown remains on the private runtime Unix socket with mode-0600 socket, mode-0700 parent and a peer-UID check against EOS; IsOS Supervisor is the intended caller. The HTTP bridge cannot forward `shutdown`, and a non-owner peer cannot terminate EOS or stop an adapter. R06 separately proves actuator stop before MuJoCo termination/reap; process-group SIGTERM alone is not that proof. Their historical types may remain parseable and their local tests may use in-process helpers, but no deployed caller can use them as a second actuator path. R01 evidence queues never carry command, result, fixed safety or stop payloads, and a full evidence queue cannot discard a command. `primitive_typed` remains the R07 browser bridge path, but its R03 side must consume the common admission envelope rather than call an older ungoverned helper. R07 owns browser Origin/CORS, revision display and correlated `InProgress` result follow-up.

## Verification boundary

The [frozen cases](../../../../tests/fixtures/r03_admission_revision/cases.json) cover managed revision provenance, stale and missing revision, missing and wrong grants, exact binding and typed limits, fake-clock health/observation/deadline, blocked queue recheck, duplicate and conflicting identity, journal faults and restart, concurrent stop, and every legacy bypass. Unit tests use a fake clock and one-call adapter; boundary tests use a real Rust listener/private socket and a stub adapter. The EOS process test constructs a schema-2 snapshot and verifier fixture; the post-merge IsOS issuance and launch handoff passed their separate boundary replay, recorded in the first-use page. Physical controller deduplication and simulator stop/reap timing remain R05/R06 acceptance, so R03 tests must not claim those properties from a mock.
