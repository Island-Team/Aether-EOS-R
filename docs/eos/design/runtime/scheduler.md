# Routed connection scheduling

[中文](../../../../docs_cn/eos/design/runtime/scheduler_cn.md) · [Graph contract](https://github.com/Island-Team/isos/blob/master/docs/isos/interfaces/node-graph.md) · [Runtime foundation](foundation.md)

**R01 implementation.** A graph edge names one producer output and one consumer input. IsOS owns `priority` and `frequency_hz` in saved bytes; EOS owns delivery after both endpoints are admitted. The source port's `expected_rate_hz` is a hint, never a cap. The [hardware interface](../../interfaces/hardware.md#r01-live-eos-local-evidence-routes--target-contract) owns the target status fields and bounds.

## Admission and ownership

G06 resolves canonical paths and exact type/port/unit/transport identities, cardinality, references and cycles; IsOS validates the whole graph and EOS revalidates its projection. R01's sole current live pair is `eos.hardware.camera@v0.1.frame` → `eos.interface.camera_gate@v0.1.frame` (`camera_frame`, image, in-process). It uses the existing camera capture and gate consumer. Other admitted EOS edges remain visible as `not_routed` or `fixed_not_scheduled`; EGIA and bridge edges are outside EOS's table. No registry suffix or data-class resemblance grants a route. Fixed safety, actuator, result, health and stop paths never enter the scheduler, accept user QoS, or create a second Dispatcher admission path.

## Delivery algorithm

An optional finite positive `frequency_hz` starts with one delivery token. After consuming it, a new token needs positive elapsed monotonic nanoseconds. For `rate <= 1e9`, compare `(elapsed_ns as f64) * rate >= 1e9`; this product is bounded below `1.85e28` for `u64` nanoseconds. For `rate > 1e9`, permit one token at the next distinct nanosecond tick. Do not calculate `1 / rate` or convert its period into an integer duration: the G06 wire admits positive subnormal values and the largest finite `f64`. A backward observation leaves the last-consumption timestamp unchanged. Consume the token on each consumer attempt, including a failed attempt, and reset the consumption timestamp to that attempt; never bank more than one token or burst after a stall. Without the field, there is no edge cap. Waiting samples remain queued under the class policy; a scheduler visit with queued work and no token increments `cap_wait_count`, not `gap_count`. The producer's own ceiling and adapter safety limits remain independent. Aggregate diagnostics instead of logging each sample.

Ready edges share weighted deficit round robin in bytewise route-key order. A **service slice** visits at most 64 distinct routes and makes at most 64 delivery attempts; the next slice resumes at a persistent cursor pointing after the last visited route. A budget exhaustion never resets the cursor to the first key. A complete round is a traversal of every ready route once, possibly spanning many slices. Nice-like `priority` in `[-20,19]` maps to positive `weight = 20 - priority` (`40..1`). On its visit a ready route gains `weight` units of credit, capped at 1024; one unit is 64 KiB of serialized payload, and a delivery costs `max(1,ceil(bytes/65536))` units, capped at 1024. It can make multiple attempts while it has credit and the slice has attempt budget; after the visit the cursor advances even if credit remains or the attempt budget was exhausted. Unused credit carries to the next round; an empty queue discards credit. A capped route with no token gets one `cap_wait_count` visit, consumes no credit, and is skipped until its next round visit. With 65 or 256 continuously ready one-unit routes at weight one, all are visited within two or four slices respectively; with arbitrary positive weights, every continuously ready route is visited within at most 256 slices because each nonempty slice advances over at least one distinct route. A weight-one maximal payload accumulates enough credit after at most 1024 visits, while higher weights can make proportionally more attempts. The loop never uses OS thread priority or promises real-time scheduling. Synthetic state/event tests exercise fairness without registering new graph routes.

Image and observation retain one latest sample per route; a newer accepted sample replaces it and increments `replaced_count` and `gap_count`. State and event use FIFO bounded at 64 records and 1 MiB serialized payload bytes per route. Overflow, including an oversized record, rejects the newest sample while preserving earlier order and increments `overflow_count` and `gap_count`. At most 256 live routes and 64 MiB of queued payload bytes are allowed globally; route-count excess rejects startup before workers, and later global-byte pressure rejects the newest sample with overflow and gap counts, preserving a previously retained latest sample. Check the net byte change before replacing that sample. A consumer error removes that evidence record, increments `consumer_error_count` and `gap_count`, and never claims delivery or auto-retries. All counters saturate at `u64::MAX` and reset only for a new runtime generation. Command and opaque data never enter these queues.

R01 stop atomically closes evidence admission and fences in-flight publication. Before draining a potentially large queue or waiting on any evidence worker, the runtime invokes Dispatcher `emergency_stop` for admitted actuators and records any stop failure. It then cancels queued records. Each route has at most one in-flight consumer acceptance. That in-flight operation receives a cancellation token and must finish or expire within 100 ms; acceptance before cancellation counts as delivered, while cancellation or deadline expiry counts exactly once as cancelled and a gap. A generation fence rejects late publication after cancellation, including an image that would otherwise appear in `VisionService::capture`. `queue_depth` excludes in-flight work. Joining evidence workers has a 1 s deadline; expiry is a visible stop failure and cannot be reported as a completed join. R01 tests check this local ordering and accounting. R06 will verify the enclosing simulator stop, termination and reap path; R01 alone does not prove physical safe stop.

The 100 ms acceptance deadline applies to the gate consumer, which uses a cancellable slot lock. A camera adapter capture is a separate synchronous producer call and may block; shutdown fences its later publication, waits up to 1 s for evidence workers, and exits with failure if a worker remains blocked. Rust cannot kill that blocked thread or claim that it joined. The existing adapter host timeout and R06 simulator stop/reap remain separate owner boundaries.

A gate consumer prepares a frame without changing its visible slot. Under the same scheduler lock used by shutdown admission sealing, the final deadline and cancellation check commits the frame, delivered generation, counter, and acceptance timestamp together. Shutdown seals the scheduler before Dispatcher `emergency_stop` and drains queues only afterward; a paused producer cannot enqueue after the seal. Camera sampling computes a saturating `Duration` for every admitted finite positive `max_rate_hz`, including subnormal values, and polls stop at 100 ms intervals while waiting.

## VisionService integration seam

`crates/aether-runtime/src/bin/aether-runtime/vision.rs` retains one capture worker per camera source. The admitted route table sits between capture and each `@v0.1` gate slot. It serializes the frame once outside the scheduler lock, then shares one immutable `Arc<CameraFrame>` across the exact declared gate routes; each route applies its own cap and queue. The locked fanout clones only `Arc` handles, including when the 64 MiB quota rejects later routes. Only a successful route consumer acceptance publishes to a gate-specific latest slot and increments that gate's delivered generation. `VisionService::capture` for an R01 gate reads that slot and generation, so a waiting, replaced, dropped or consumer-failed frame cannot appear through the browser preview. Two gates sharing one camera may have different caps and generations without multiplying camera captures. Gate health still comes from Dispatcher status and health, not route counters.

The existing `@v1.0` gate's direct preview path remains a legacy compatibility behavior until migration. It is not an R01 graph route, does not accept user QoS, and appears as `not_routed` when both its edge endpoints are EOS-local. Do not apply its direct path to new `@v0.1` gates. The runtime builds and snapshots `route_status` from the live route table in the owner-only Unix socket handler in `aether-runtime.rs`. It does not infer delivery from `VisionService::active_gates` or a log line. It includes only edges whose source and target are both EOS-local. In particular, the admitted legacy EGIA guard → EOS Dispatcher `DesignOnly` validation edge is absent; IsOS presents that cross-core declaration. The socket rejects or closes `route_status` once shutdown begins, so no `stopped` row is promised. Stop follows the bounded evidence and Dispatcher ordering above; R06 owns later simulator sequencing.

## Diagnostics and acceptance

The owner-only runtime socket target `route_status` returns validated EOS-local declarations, ordered by key, with no payload. Active rows expose configured QoS, queue depth, delivered/wait/loss counters and last successful monotonic delivery; local design-only and fixed rows have null scheduling values, `routed:false`, and no inferred health. Cross-core declarations are omitted. IsOS remains the deployment-status authority. Tests cover exact-route admission, similar but unrouted types, fake-clock caps including extreme finite values and backward time, 65/256-route cursor progress, latest replacement, FIFO/global saturation, consumer error, bounded in-flight stop and emergency-stop ordering, and the invariant that no edge creates a second actuator route.
